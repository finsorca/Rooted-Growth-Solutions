The Challenge

A Critical Gap in Smallholder Support

South Africa's 250 000 smallholder farmers are the backbone of rural livelihoods, yet they remain underserved by a system that supports them in isolation — technical advice here, financial training there — but never both together.

NGOs, government institutions, and private organisations provide support in fragments. The missing link is an integrated approach that connects where a farmer works and how that translates into profit or loss.

Without that connection, planning decisions suffer, resources are wasted, and farmers struggle to grow sustainable enterprises — despite access to land, labour, and markets.

No Structured Financial Systems

Most smallholder farmers lack access to farm-level financial tools — enterprise budgets, cash flow plans, or cost structures that help them understand profitability.

Limited Farm-Level Data

Decisions are made without reliable spatial or production data — leaving farmers unable to identify underperforming zones or optimise land use.

Disconnected Planning & Finance

Even when support exists, spatial analysis and financial management are delivered in isolation — creating plans that look good on paper but don't survive contact with the season.

Poor Resource Allocation

Without knowing which land units are productive and at what cost, farmers spread inputs too thin or invest where returns are low — eroding margins over time.

How We Work

A Practical, Step-by-Step Methodology

01

Engage & Assess

We begin with each farmer — understanding their land, enterprise mix, challenges, and goals. This baseline shapes everything that follows.

02

Map the Farm

Using QGIS, we produce accurate farm maps — boundaries, production zones, infrastructure, and land utilisation — giving farmers a clear picture of their spatial assets.

03

Build the Numbers

Enterprise budgets, cost structures, and cash flow projections are developed — creating a financial baseline that connects to land and production decisions.

04

Integrate & Plan

We bring spatial and financial data together into a practical farm business plan — aligning land use with the enterprises that make financial sense.

05

Monitor & Improve

Simple reporting tools and regular check-ins ensure farmers stay on track — and that programme partners can demonstrate measurable impact.

Phase 1

The Pilot Programme

Our first phase focuses on a carefully selected group of 10 to 20 smallholder farmers across KwaZulu-Natal. This pilot is designed to test, refine, and prove the integrated model before scaling it through programme partnerships.

Every element of the pilot — the farm assessments, spatial mapping, financial analysis, and performance tracking — is designed to be practical, replicable, and scalable. The model that emerges will be available to NGOs, commodity organisations, corporates, and government bodies looking to support smallholders at scale.
